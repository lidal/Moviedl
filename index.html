              How to play

              One film a day. You never see its title — only its five top-billed actors, one per round.

              The reveal is rigged, on purpose

              Actors arrive in order of how unpredictable their careers are. Round 1 is a wildcard whose filmography runs from masterpiece to direct-to-video, and tells you almost nothing. Each round after that the cast gets steadier, until round 5 hands you its most typecast member — whose films cluster tightly enough to actually be worth something.

              The picture sharpens as the money gets cheaper. You are paid most for betting when you know least.

              The call

              Each round you slide to a rating and put chips behind it, or sit out. You have 100 chips for the whole film and can stake at most 50 in any one round, so you always have a decision left after the first one.

              The dial opens on what the cast you have seen so far averages out to — weighted toward the steadier actors, because they are the ones whose presence predicts anything. It is an anchor, not a hint: most films miss it, and the whole game is guessing which way.

              How close is close enough

              The green band on the dial is the window that profits. Land dead centre and you win your full stake; land on its edge and you break even; miss by twice the band and you lose the lot. You cannot lose more than that, however wild the miss.

              You never have to work this out. Drag the marker and watch the band — it is the scoring rule, drawn.

              The band tightens every round

              Round 1 forgives ±2.00. Round 5 forgives only ±1.00. By the last round you have seen the whole cast, so the game asks for real precision in exchange.

              Early conviction is worth more

              Round 1 chips swing the scoreboard ×3.0; by round 5 they swing ×1.0. That multiplier cuts both ways — an early call that is wrong costs triple too.

              Between the shrinking band and the falling multiplier, the same accuracy is always worth more the earlier you commit to it. Waiting for certainty is how you lose slowly.

              Backing yourself, or changing your mind

              The dial stays where you left it, so calling the same number again — doubling down — takes no effort. Moving it is the deliberate act.

              And you do not have to pick one. Put a call either side of where you think the truth sits and, if both land inside their bands, both of them pay. That is a bracket, and it is the best thing that can happen to you.

              Ratings and calls both sit on the same 0.1 grid, so every ticket resolves to an exact number of credits. Nothing is ever a coin flip you cannot see coming.
