              How to play

              One film a day. You never see its title — only its five top-billed actors, one per round.

              The reveal is rigged, on purpose

              Actors arrive in order of how unpredictable their careers are. Round 1 is a wildcard whose filmography runs from masterpiece to direct-to-video, and tells you almost nothing. Each round after that the cast gets steadier, until round 5 hands you its most typecast member — whose films cluster tightly enough to actually be worth something.

              The picture sharpens as the money gets cheaper. You are paid most for betting when you know least.

              The bet

              The board posts a line — a rating, priced off the actors you have seen so far. Each round you stake chips on over or under, or take no bet. A bet wins or loses its stake; what changes is the multiplier. You have 100 chips and can stake at most 25 a round, so four bets spend the lot — you cannot empty the stack into the easy early rounds.

              The harder the bet, the bigger the prize

              Backing a side pushes the line away from it — a full 25-chip bet moves it 0.60. So betting the same way again means taking a number you made harder yourself, and you are paid for exactly that:

              R1  OVER 25 @ 6.85   ×1.00   +250
R2  OVER 25 @ 7.35   ×1.72   +430
R3  OVER 25 @ 7.65   ×2.44   +610
R4  OVER 25 @ 8.15   ×3.16   +790
R5  no bet  @ 8.75           past it — leave it alone

              “Is it over 6.85?” is easy once you know the film. “Is it over 8.15?” is a real question, and it pays three times as much.

              How far you can ride it is your guess at the number. Someone who only knows “it is great” gets off two rounds early and banks a fraction. Ride one round too far and you hand back more than the last two rounds made.

              Waiting earns you nothing extra

              The multiplier is 1 + how far your own money moved the line — not the round number. Sit out four rounds and the fifth is still ×1.00, because a bet at the opening line with the whole cast revealed is the easiest bet in the game. The last round lets you stake 50 rather than 25, so passing is a real fallback — just not a plan.

              No bet is a real move

              Round 1 is a dossier and one wildcard actor. Passing costs you nothing — a day where you back nothing scores zero, not a loss, and it will not break a streak.

              Or change your mind

              Turning around gets you a better number than you started with. Every ticket is graded against the line it was struck at, so backing over at 6.55 and under at 6.85 can pay twice if the film lands between them.

              Lines always end in .x5, so nothing ever pushes. Every ticket resolves.
