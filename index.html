            How to play

            One film a day. You never see its title — only its five top-billed actors, one per round.

            The reveal is rigged, on purpose

            Actors arrive in order of how predictable their careers are. Round 1 is the most typecast member of the cast: their films cluster tightly, so their presence tells you a lot. By round 5 you are looking at a wildcard whose filmography runs from masterpiece to direct-to-video, and tells you almost nothing.

            Your best information arrives first. So does your best price.

            The bet

            The board posts a line — a rating, priced off what this cast usually appears in. Each round you stake chips on over or under, or sit out. You have 100 chips for the whole film and can stake at most 50 in any one round.

            Early conviction is worth more

            Round 1 chips swing the scoreboard ×3.0; by round 5 they swing ×1.0. That multiplier cuts both ways — an early bet that is wrong costs triple too.

            The line moves against you

            Back over and the line climbs; back under and it drops. So doubling down later means paying a worse number for the same opinion — while turning around gets you a better one.

            Every ticket is graded against the line it was struck at, not the closing line. Bet over at 6.55, reverse to under at 6.85, and a true rating of 6.7 pays you twice. That is a middle, and it is the best thing that can happen to you.

            Lines always end in .x5, so nothing ever pushes. Every ticket resolves.
