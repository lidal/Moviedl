              How to play

              One film a day. You never see its title — only its five top-billed actors, one per round.

              The reveal is rigged, on purpose

              Actors arrive in order of how unpredictable their careers are. Round 1 is a wildcard whose filmography runs from masterpiece to direct-to-video, and tells you almost nothing. Each round after that the cast gets steadier, until round 5 hands you its most typecast member — whose films cluster tightly enough to actually be worth something.

              The picture sharpens as the money gets cheaper. You are paid most for betting when you know least.

              The bet

              The board posts a line — a rating, priced off the actors you have seen so far, leaning on the steady ones because they are the only ones that predict anything. Each round you stake chips on over or under, or take no bet. You have 100 chips and can stake at most 50 in a round — except the last, where you may back everything you have left.

              No bet is a real move

              Round 1 is a dossier and one wildcard actor. Often there is simply nothing to read, and passing costs you only that round’s multiplier — your chips keep their full value, and a day where you backed nothing scores zero, not a loss. It will not break a streak either.

              You are never asked to guess a film you have no way of knowing. If the price is not worth taking, do not take it.

              Early conviction is worth a little more

              Round 1 chips swing the scoreboard ×2.0; by round 5 they swing ×1.0. That multiplier cuts both ways — an early bet that is wrong costs double too.

              The line moves, for two reasons

              Each new name reprices it: a prestige actor lifts the line, a straight-to-video regular drops it. On top of that, your own money pushes it away from the side you back — so doubling down later means paying a worse number for the same opinion, while turning around gets you a better one.

              Every ticket is graded against the line it was struck at, not the closing line. Bet over at 6.55, reverse to under at 6.85, and a true rating of 6.7 pays you twice. That is a middle. Watch the repricing though — a big name landing between your two bets can carry the line past both of them.

              Lines always end in .x5, so nothing ever pushes. Every ticket resolves.
